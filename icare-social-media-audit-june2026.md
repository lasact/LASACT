## Section 1: Who Is ICARE — Mission & Brand Foundation

**Full name:** I CARE — East Baton Rouge Parish School System Alcohol, Drug Abuse, and Violence Prevention Program

**Founded:** 1982 — over 40 years serving Baton Rouge students and families

**Mission:** To provide prevention education in the areas of alcohol, tobacco, other drugs, and violence to all students and families within the Baton Rouge schools and community — equipping Pre-K through 12th grade students and families with the knowledge and skills to make healthy lifestyle choices.

**Staff:** 16 Licensed Prevention Professionals — career educators with master's degrees and 5+ years classroom teaching experience. Every public school in EBR Parish visited weekly.

**Services:**
- Individual and family consultation
- Small group student support (especially trauma/grief)
- Classroom education (PreK–12)
- Staff and parent trainings
- Prevention campaign support
- Social-emotional learning programs

**Topics covered:**
- Vaping and tobacco prevention
- Alcohol and drug abuse prevention
- Bullying awareness and prevention
- Violence prevention
- Cyber safety
- Conflict resolution
- Mindfulness
- Leadership development
- Grief support

**Summer 2026 (just launched):** Free presentations available to summer camps, faith-based groups, and community organizations. Topics: vaping prevention, bullying awareness, cyber safety, conflict resolution, mindfulness, leadership development.

**ICARE Summer Showcase** — annual event featured on EBR Schools Facebook

**Two audiences:**
1. **Students (PreK–12)** — primarily reached through school programming; social content should speak to middle/high schoolers
2. **Parents and families** — the primary social media audience; need tools, awareness, and connection to resources

### Facebook — ICAREProgramEBR

**What's working:**
- Community-facing platform with the right audience (parents, teachers, community members)
- Historical content base likely exists
- ICARE Summer Showcase was shared via EBR Schools' main Facebook — shows cross-posting potential
- Facebook Groups culture aligns with parent support communities

**What's not working / gaps:**
- Facebook's organic reach has dropped to 2–6% for pages — without a consistent video strategy (Reels/native video), posts get minimal distribution
- No evidence of a consistent posting schedule
- No Facebook Group to build a parent community vs. just broadcasting
- Parent engagement requires conversation, not just information — static posts underperform

**Recommendations:**
- Post 4–5x per week (mix of Reels, carousels, and text posts)
- Create a private "ICARE Parent Community" Facebook Group for deeper engagement
- Use Facebook's native video upload (not YouTube links) for maximum reach
- Cross-post every TikTok video natively to Facebook Reels
- Tag EBR Schools, school PTAs, and community partners in relevant posts

### TikTok — @icareebrschools

**Known content:**
- "What Recovery Looks Like: Matthew's Story" — personal recovery story video (HIGH value format)
- "LIVE A VAPE FREE LIFE" with #mentalhealthawareness — prevention messaging
- "Bullying Awareness: Join the Fight Against Aggressive..." — awareness photo post
- "Starting off strong this holiday season" — seasonal content

**What's working:**
- Recovery story format (Matthew's Story) — this is the highest-performing format for prevention content. Personal narratives drive emotional connection and shares
- On-topic content aligned with mission
- Using hashtags like #mentalhealthawareness

**What's not working / gaps:**
- Photo posts on TikTok significantly underperform video — TikTok is a video-first platform; photo carousels get a fraction of the reach
- Posting frequency appears inconsistent — TikTok rewards accounts that post 1–4x per day
- No clear hook strategy — prevention content competes with entertainment; the first 1–2 seconds determine everything
- Content likely skews informational rather than relatable/emotional — "edutainment" is what works for this age group
- No duet/stitch strategy — TikTok's interactive features (duets, stitches, challenges) dramatically expand reach

**Recommendations:**
- Post minimum 5x per week on TikTok; ideally daily
- Every post must be video (not photo)
- Lead every video with a hook in the first 2 seconds ("Did you know your teen's vape contains more nicotine than a cigarette?" / "This is what bullying actually does to the brain...")
- Film more student story content (with permission) — authentic faces build trust and shares
- Use trending sounds for non-serious content (day-in-the-life, behind the scenes)
- Add captions/text overlay to ALL videos — most watched with no sound

### Instagram — @icareebr

**Known content:**
- Photo and video posts covering prevention topics
- An "ICARE EBR Summit" was attended and posted about
- Reels exist

**What's working:**
- Instagram Reels are the primary driver of reach for non-follower discovery
- Visual platform well-suited for infographics, student spotlights, and quote cards
- Summit/event content shows real-world presence

**What's not working / gaps:**
- Instagram Reels need to be vertical (9:16) and under 90 seconds to maximize reach — unclear if all videos meet this
- No evidence of Instagram Stories strategy — Stories are where daily engagement lives
- Instagram's algorithm favors accounts that use all features (Feed, Reels, Stories, Lives)
- Parent audience on Instagram expects polished, emotionally resonant visuals — educational content needs to feel warm, not clinical

**Recommendations:**
- Post 1 Reel + 3–5 Stories per day
- Reels = awareness and growth; Stories = community and engagement
- Use Stories for interactive content: polls ("Would you know if your child was vaping?"), quizzes, Q&A
- Create a Highlights library: Vaping Facts, Bullying Resources, Parent Tools, Summer Programs
- Cross-post all TikTok videos as Reels (remove TikTok watermark first)

## Section 3: Industry Research — Marketing to Kids & Parents

### Marketing to Parents

**What parents respond to on social media:**
- **Fear + solution** — "Here's a danger your child faces + here's how we help" is the highest-converting frame for prevention programs
- **Real stories** — Parent testimonials and student success stories outperform statistics
- **Practical tools** — "3 questions to ask your teen about vaping" gets saved and shared; abstract awareness posts do not
- **Community** — Parents want to know other parents face the same challenges; community content (polls, questions) drives comments
- **School-year timing** — Parent engagement spikes at: back to school, report card periods, before major events (prom, homecoming, summer)

**Platforms parents use most (2026):**
1. Facebook — primary platform for 30–50 age group; parent groups and community pages
2. Instagram — growing, especially 28–40 demographic
3. TikTok — parents are now the fastest-growing demographic on TikTok

**Parent trust signals:**
- Credentials and program longevity ("40+ years serving Baton Rouge students")
- Affiliation with EBR Schools (huge credibility for local parents)
- Staff faces and names — parents trust people, not programs
- Data/outcomes ("Every EBR public school visited every week")

---

### Marketing to Kids and Teens

**What works for teens (middle/high school) on social media:**
- **Authenticity over polish** — teens distrust anything that feels like an adult trying to be cool; peer voices are more trusted than authority voices
- **Entertainment-first** — if it's not interesting in 2 seconds, they scroll; lead with story, not message
- **Humor and relatability** — "this is literally my school" content gets shared; preachy content gets skipped
- **Empowerment, not fear** — "you're stronger than the pressure" lands better than "drugs will ruin your life"
- **Student-generated content** — teens engaging with the account as participants, not audience

**TikTok-specific for teens:**
- Trending audio dramatically increases reach even for non-entertainment content
- Duet and stitch challenges (e.g., "stitch this with your coping skill") create participation
- Behind-the-scenes of ICARE programs humanizes the team
- "POV" format works well for empathy content ("POV: Your friend just asked you to vape")

## Section 5: Brand Voice & Tone

**Voice:**
- Warm and approachable, not clinical or preachy
- Empowering, not fear-based (for student-facing content)
- Trustworthy and credible, not corporate (for parent-facing content)
- Real and human — staff faces, student voices, community moments

**Tone by platform:**
- **TikTok:** Conversational, energetic, relatable, slightly informal. Teen-adjacent without trying too hard.
- **Instagram:** Warm and visually clean. Inspiring. Slightly more polished than TikTok.
- **Facebook:** Community-focused, informational, resource-driven. Speaks directly to parents.

**Phrases that work:**
- "Your kid needs to hear this."
- "40+ years keeping Baton Rouge students safe."
- "Real talk about [topic]."
- "Tools for parents. Skills for students."

**Avoid:**
- Overly clinical or government-agency language
- Heavy statistics without a human story attached
- Shame-based messaging ("if your child does X...")
- Generic inspirational quotes without ICARE context

## Section 7: Analytics — What to Track

Set up monthly reporting on these metrics across all platforms:

| Metric | Platform | Goal |
|--------|---------|------|
| Reach / Impressions | All | Are we being discovered? |
| Follower growth (monthly) | All | Is the audience growing? |
| Engagement rate | All | TikTok target: 3%+ / IG: 1%+ / FB: 0.5%+ |
| Video views (completion rate) | TikTok, Reels | % watching past 3 seconds = hook working |
| Saves | Instagram | Highest-value action — content worth keeping |
| Shares | Facebook, TikTok | Content worth spreading |
| Link clicks | Facebook | Parents clicking to request programs |
| Top performing post/format | All | Double down on what works |

**Monthly review questions:**
1. Which post got the most reach this month? What made it work?
2. Which post got the most saves/shares? That's your best content format.
3. What day/time drove the highest engagement?
4. Did we grow followers? If not, which posts were Reel/TikTok and which were static?
